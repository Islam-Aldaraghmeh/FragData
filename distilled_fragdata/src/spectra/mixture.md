# Experimental spectral mixtures

`fit_spectral_mixture(predictions, target, min_candidates=2)` fits a nonnegative
combination of existing candidate predictions to one measured MS3 spectrum.
The gallery enables it with `fit_mixture=True`, independently for each MS3 that
remains after collision-energy filtering. It does not run Glacier again.

```python
plot_fragments_with_spectra(
    filtered_bags, predicted_spectra,
    fit_mixture=True, mixture_min_candidates=2,
    ms3_collision_energies=[40], only_with_ms3=True,
)
```

Candidate cards show their coefficients for the corresponding MS3. A visible
preview above the scrolling candidate rows shows each fitted spectrum, its
aligned cosine, and the measured MS3. Each mixture card also shows the best
single-candidate score on that same fitting axis.
Existing individual sparse cosine scores and candidate ordering remain intact.

## Definition of the fit

1. Copy and normalize each usable prediction to total intensity one. Missing
   and empty predictions are excluded and receive zero in the returned weight
   vector; the gallery labels these coefficients unavailable.
2. Construct a common axis using predicted masses only. Sort all predicted
   peaks and group them using fixed anchors and the existing tolerance
   `max(0.005 Da, max(m1, m2) * 20e-6)`. Each bin retains its lowest mass;
   candidate intensities in the bin are summed. Bins never move with fitted
   weights or with the experimental target.
3. Sum-merge experimental peaks without thresholding or a peak-count limit,
   normalize them, and use the existing greedy one-to-one mass matcher against
   that axis. Append unmatched target peaks as additional rows. Unmatched
   prediction peaks remain in the matrix, so both kinds of unexplained signal
   penalize the cosine.
4. Solve `min ||A w - b||²` with `w >= 0` using SciPy NNLS. The nonzero fitted
   vector is the projection of `b` onto the cone generated by the candidate
   columns. Its direction maximizes cosine with `b` on this fixed axis.
5. Divide the coefficients by their sum. Cosine is scale invariant, so this
   preserves the optimum while making every coefficient lie in `[0, 1]` and
   their sum equal one. The plotted mixture is precisely this weighted sum of
   the aligned, normalized candidates. No top-k filter is applied.

The aligned-fit cosine is an experimental metric. Pooling and fixed mass
alignment can differ from independently matching each original spectrum near
tolerance boundaries. Compare its improvement with `best_single_cosine`, not
with an individual candidate's original sparse score. Coefficients describe a
fit to the observed data; they are not candidate probabilities or measured
chemical abundances. Collinear predictions may have nonunique coefficients.

## Results and verification

The result contains `status`, `reason`, a full-length `weights` array indexed by
original candidate position, `usable_indices`, `spectrum`, `cosine`, and
`best_single_cosine`. Status is `ok`, `insufficient_candidates`, `empty_target`,
`no_overlap`, or `solver_failed`. Uninformative or failed fits return an empty
spectrum and an explicit explanation.

Focused tests recover analytic two- and three-component mixtures, check
unmatched-peak penalties, preserve all peaks and source arrays, exercise
collinear/missing candidates and optimizer failures, and verify the gallery's
per-MS3 coefficients, filtering, and default-off behavior.
